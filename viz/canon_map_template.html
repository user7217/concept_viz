How to read this

Every piece of mathematics your robot's code depends on, and the chain connecting the two. It opens on the whole program and goes down only where you ask it to.

Start here

One circle, agrios_ws. Click it and its three subsystems appear. Keep clicking down:

program → subsystem → component (a node that actually runs) → algorithm → the mathematics

The +N badge counts what is still hidden beneath a circle, so you can see where more is without showing it. The breadcrumb along the top is your trail back, and every step of it is clickable.

What the shapes mean
  • Size is depth in the hierarchy. The program is largest, the floor of the mathematics smallest.
  • Colour is what a node is: system and subsystem, component, algorithm, concept, and technique — a move you perform rather than an object you know.
  • A ring means the node is on your current learning path.
  • Lines point from a result down to something it needs.
  • Dashed arrows between components are data: a topic one sends and the other reads. Click one to see where both ends are written in your code and config.
  • Faint components run in your program but have no mathematics of their own here; data passes through them.
The axes are real measurements

Each axis is bound to a quantity, named on the viewport, and selecting a node drops a dashed marker onto every axis showing its reading. Swap them in the sidebar:

  • walkthrough level — how far below the program it sits
  • reuse — how many of the 20 algorithms need it. High reuse and low cost is what you learn first.
  • cost — how many prerequisites it drags in with it
  • prerequisite depth, tier, your profile, steps, exercises

Read one axis at a time. The diagonal between two nodes mixes units and means nothing, which is why nothing here invites you to read it. An axis set to spread has no line, because that direction carries no information at all. Positions are spaced by rank, not by magnitude — most of these measures are so skewed that proportional spacing crushes everything into one corner.

Clicking things
  • A circle opens its panel and reveals what it needs.
  • A line tells you why that prerequisite exists — the real reason, written when the edge was made, not the word "requires".
  • Arrow keys walk the graph: ↓ to what this node needs, ↑ to what needs it, ← → across its siblings. F flies back to the selection.
  • Drag turns the graph, two fingers pan, pinch zooms about the cursor, shift-drag pans. + − 0 also work. Esc closes.
  • Drag the panel's left edge to resize it; the width is remembered.
  • On a phone: one finger turns, two fingers pinch and pan. Tap a circle and its panel rises from the bottom; drag the handle up to read, down to close. The controls are behind ☰.
What a panel holds

Why the node is on the path, what it does in your codebase, the config parameters it governs, the statement itself, and the real code that executes it — from your repo and from the library.

Not every node has all of it. A node with steps 0 is a definition: there is genuinely nothing to derive, and that is the honest answer rather than a gap. An empty project section means nothing in your code reaches that node yet.
